The situation

The company teaches IT and AI skills to working adults in the United States. Creator marketing was run by two people: the head of the function and me. Partway through, the company moved from career-change bootcamps to a new line of AI programs, so the audience, the creators and the offer all had to change.

There was no team to hand the routine to. Finding creators, checking whether their audience fits, writing to them, answering, negotiating and getting contracts signed is hundreds of small tasks a day. I built the machine that does those tasks and kept the decisions for people.

What I ran, in order

  1. Find

    Look-alike searches from creators who had already worked, creators tagged by similar brands, past partners, agency rosters. A search run cost $3 to $15.

  2. Check the fit

    Each creator is judged on what they say in their videos, not on their bio. Transcripts go through a scoring model trained on our own yes and no decisions.

  3. Write and follow up

    Email, two creator marketplaces and platform messages land in one inbox. Addresses are checked before sending, and each mailbox has a daily limit.

  4. Negotiate

    Replies are sorted and the quoted price is pulled out, including from attached rate cards. The price is compared with reach measured from the creator's recent posts.

  5. Contract and brief

    Statements of work, usage rights, payment steps with finance and legal, then the creative brief.

Numbers that held up when checked

  • 8,700

    creators in the pipeline, each with fit score, contact route and conversation history.

  • 9% to 33%

    reply rate depending on the channel. Creator marketplaces replied best, on small samples.

  • 45 overnight

    The request was for 20 more expert candidates. The list was ready the next morning and the run cost under $3.

  • 0.72 → 0.86

    accuracy of the fit model (AUC) after adding video transcripts. At 90% recall it blocks 62% of creators who would not fit.

  • 17 hours → 3 minutes

    from a teammate's message to a prepared answer waiting for my approval. Seventeen hours was the worst case before.

  • 2.4% → about 1%

  • 28 of 47

    reply drafts were better with the new drafting engine in a blind review; 6 were worse.

  • 118 fixed

    serious findings from a full audit of the system, which now has more than 2,100 automated tests.

Three calls that saved money

Measure the reach before discussing the price

A creator asked for $52k. The system showed almost no reach, so the team was about to drop them. I went back to the raw posts: old pinned videos and videos too new to have collected views were distorting the sample. With the method fixed, the real reach was 17 times higher. The quote still worked out to a $1,120 CPM, so the answer stayed no, for the right reason. The same check turned other creators from a no into a good deal.

Go back to creators who already delivered

Cold search is slow and expensive. I assembled the creators whose earlier videos had performed and offered to repeat the work, starting from the earlier fee. The first four asked for 0.6 to 1.1 times what they had been paid before.

Re-read the contacts nobody wrote to

The database held 6,174 creators who had been filtered out on thin data and never contacted. I re-scored all of them on their actual videos and recovered 194 strong fits for about a dollar of data collection plus model time.

Where the agents stop

  • A person presses Send on every one-to-one message.
  • A person accepts every price, budget and contract term.
  • One draft per conversation, and a message counts as sent only when the mailbox says so.
  • Text written by other people is data for the agents, never an instruction.

Things went wrong along the way. Each time, the fix was a rule in code, not a promise to be careful.

What went wrongThe rule it became
Internal sourcing notes were emailed to 45 creators.A message that does not read like a pitch is held, not sent.
A cleanup job deleted replies that had already gone out.Only the mailbox's own sent label decides what was sent.
Drafts tried to reopen prices that were already agreed.A price agreed in writing is closed; the checker rejects any draft that changes it.
The mail provider blocked a second sending domain after 894 sends and three replies.No third cold touch, and no jump from zero to hundreds of sends a week.
