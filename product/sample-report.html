Integration Audit: Brambleway Academy

Sample report. Built from the synthetic dataset in data/sample/. The advertiser, the creators and every number are invented.

Offline mode: tables and readout are produced by code with fixed rules. No LLM wrote this text. The LLM mode adds a fuller narrative on top of the same tables.

1. What was analysed

  • Integrations: 118 (youtube 58, reel 40, story 12, tiktok 8)
  • With content scores (transcript available): 88
  • With at least one contact: 117; with at least one purchase: 65
  • Valid cost per contact: 117

Two layers are kept apart. Layer 1 covers what the creator's content can plausibly move: traffic, contacts and cost per contact. Layer 2 covers deals, calls and purchases, which depend on sales operations.

2. Executive summary

  1. On short-form (Reels and TikTok), a higher benefit_clarity score goes with a lower cost per contact (Spearman rho -0.41, medium effect, N=35, power 0.22, underpowered; table C2v2). Probable signal.
  2. On YouTube, a higher specificity score goes with a lower cost per contact (Spearman rho -0.36, medium effect, N=52, power 0.25, underpowered; table C1v2). Probable signal.
  3. Median cost per contact by platform: story $33 (n=12); youtube $83 (n=58); reel $87 (n=40); tiktok $208 (n=8). This compares funnel metrics only, never content features across platforms (table R1v2). Hypothesis.
  4. After the click, the weakest funnel stage is Calls -> Purchase with a median conversion of 8.8% (table R2v2). That stage belongs to the sales conversation and pricing, not to the creator's script. Hypothesis (descriptive only).
  5. Share of integrations with at least one purchase: reel 42.5% of 40; story 75.0% of 12; tiktok 25.0% of 8; youtube 63.8% of 58. Purchases sit several steps after the content, so this is context for sales operations, not a verdict on creative (table D1v2). Probable signal.

4. Limitations

  • 38 statistical tests were run. P-values are adjusted with Benjamini-Hochberg inside each table family, and the badges use the adjusted values.
  • 32 of the tested rows have power below 0.50 at the observed effect size. Treat them as ideas to test, not as findings.
  • Platforms are never pooled for content features. YouTube reads and short-form reads are scored and compared separately, because length, placement and audience intent differ too much.
  • The data is observational. Creators were not assigned scripts at random, so a pattern can come from the creator's audience rather than from the script.
  • Content scores come from an LLM rater. Scores that are unstable between repeated runs should not carry a conclusion (see the multi-run step).
  • Small groups in this dataset: story (n=12), tiktok (n=8). Their rows are descriptive only.

5. What to test next

  1. Brief half of the next short-form (Reels and TikTok) creators to raise benefit_clarity and leave the other half on the standard brief. Hypothesis: it lowers cost per contact. Needs about 96 integrations per arm for 80% power at the observed effect. Primary metric: cost per contact.
  2. Brief half of the next YouTube creators to raise specificity and leave the other half on the standard brief. Hypothesis: it lowers cost per contact. Needs about 123 integrations per arm for 80% power at the observed effect. Primary metric: cost per contact.
  3. Brief half of the next YouTube creators to raise benefit_clarity and leave the other half on the standard brief. Hypothesis: it lowers cost per contact. Needs about 128 integrations per arm for 80% power at the observed effect. Primary metric: cost per contact.
